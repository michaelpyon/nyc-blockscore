# PERSONA.md: nyc-blockscore

## The person

**Maya Torres, 29. Product analyst at a fintech near Union Square. Lease ends August 31.**

She has narrowed her apartment hunt to 3 units: a 1BR on Driggs Ave in Greenpoint, a 1BR off Franklin Ave in Crown Heights, and a long-shot walkup on Orchard St in the Lower East Side. All 3 are within $150 of each other in rent. The apartments themselves are fine. The blocks are the open question, and she knows it, because her current place taught her the hard way: she signed above a bar with a smoking patio and has filed 2 noise complaints herself.

## The exact job she hires this product for

"Settle a 2-way or 3-way tie between specific blocks, tonight, in bed, before the broker calls back tomorrow." Not neighborhood research. Not browsing. She has addresses in hand and 48 to 72 hours of decision window. She wants a defensible verdict she can paste into the group chat when her friends ask "so which one??"

Secondary job: ammunition. A concrete reason ("the Franklin block has active scaffolding and a D on noise") feels safer to act on than a vibe.

## Where she is online

- **r/NYCapartments** and **r/AskNYC** on Reddit: lurks daily during the hunt, has posted exactly once ("is this block of Franklin Ave loud?") and got 4 conflicting answers
- **NYC Housing Discord** (the one linked from r/NYCapartments): reads the #neighborhood-questions channel
- **Listings Project** email and **StreetEasy saved-search alerts**: her inbound feed
- **X**: follows @NYCTSubway complaints, urbanist posters, and the "new NYC data map just dropped" genre; this is where a link to BlockScore would actually reach her
- Group chat: iMessage thread named "maya's apt saga" with 4 friends

## What she uses today instead

1. StreetEasy for the listing, then Google Maps Street View to "walk" the block, checking for scaffolding, bars, and truck routes
2. Searching "[street name] loud" on Reddit
3. The 11pm walk-by: physically visiting the block at night, which she can do for at most 1 of the 3 finalists
4. Occasionally the NYC 311 open-data portal, which she opened once, got a CSV table, and closed

BlockScore replaces the 4-tab, 45-minute version of this with 1 screen.

## Taste: 3 named products she considers premium

1. **Citymapper**: dense, fast, opinionated, tells you THE answer (take this train) instead of showing options
2. **NYT Upshot / "Extremely Detailed Map" interactives**: dark data-journalism polish, real cartographic restraint, numbers with sources
3. **Transit App**: playful precision on a phone screen, instant load, zero onboarding

## Her vocabulary

"The block", "walk-by", "scaffolding" (she says it with dread), "the L is a lie", "quiet street vs dead street", "deal-breaker", "is this normal for the area", cross-street naming ("Bedford between N 6th and N 7th"). She never says "civic data" or "urban analytics". She says "receipts".

## Device

iPhone 15, Safari, at night, one thumb, often at 40 percent brightness in bed. Second surface: work MacBook, Chrome, lunch break. Mobile is the primary render target, full stop.

## 5-second bounce triggers

1. A map or grid that takes more than 2 seconds to paint on LTE
2. Data that smells fake: round numbers everywhere, no source, no methodology link
3. An email-capture wall or "sign up to see scores"
4. Generic AI-template landing page (purple gradient, floating 3D blobs, "Unlock insights")
5. Desktop-only layout that makes her pinch-zoom

## The unprompted-share moment

She adds her 2 finalist blocks to Compare and the verdict card renders: both blocks side by side, letter grades, 5 dimension bars, a declared winner, and the 1 dimension that broke the tie. She screenshots that card and drops it in "maya's apt saga" with "ok it's decided lol". If the copied share link unfurls on X or iMessage into a 2-up scorecard image (the dynamic OG bet), the link itself becomes the screenshot and the loop closes without her doing anything.

## Verdict on the V2 evangelist

Keep the prior persona's spine (anxious 26 to 34 NYC renter with 2 to 3 specific apartments, r/NYCapartments, NYC Housing Discord). It is correct: this is a decision tool, not a browsing tool, and the compare verdict is the product. What the card had was a demographic; this doc replaces it with 1 person at the exact moment of hire. No overrule, only sharpening: the buyer is the tie-breaker seeker in the final 72 hours, not a general "apartment hunter", and everything (IA, copy, OG cards) should serve the tie-break moment.
